# Competitive Position

## Objective

Position ADF against adjacent product categories without naming competitors.

## Background

Buyers shortlist tools by category habit. ADF must be compared on the correct axis:
structured AI engineering framework/platform — not chat UX, ticket tracking, or CI alone.

## Current Situation

ADF `1.0.0-rc1` occupies the intersection of development framework, knowledge/process
continuity, Studio control, and enterprise-oriented governance foundations.

## Analysis

Category-by-category contrast clarifies fit and non-fit. Naming vendors is unnecessary
for strategy and ages poorly; category logic is enough.

## Strategy

| Category | Typical focus | ADF position |
|----------|---------------|--------------|
| **AI coding assistants** | In-editor generation and chat productivity | ADF is not primarily an assistant chat product; it is a framework for continuous, contract-bound AI-assisted delivery |
| **Project management tools** | Tasks, sprints, status reporting | ADF does not replace PM suites; it preserves engineering/AI operating context and documentation discipline |
| **Developer platforms** | Shared services, internal portals, platform APIs | ADF aligns more closely here as a development framework with SDK/distribution surfaces, while remaining AI-engineering specific |
| **DevOps platforms** | CI/CD, environments, release automation | ADF complements delivery pipelines; it is not positioned as a full DevOps replacement |
| **Knowledge platforms** | Wikis and document stores | ADF treats knowledge as tied to architecture, runtime, and build state — not as a standalone wiki product |

**Primary category claim:** AI Development Framework / Enterprise AI Engineering Platform.

## Recommendations

1. In RFPs, reframe “assistant vs ADF” questions toward continuity, governance, and SSOT.
2. Accept non-overlap: recommend keeping PM/DevOps tools where they fit.
3. Avoid competitor name-dropping in SSOT documents.
