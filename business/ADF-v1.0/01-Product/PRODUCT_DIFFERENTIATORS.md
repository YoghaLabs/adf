# Product Differentiators

## Objective

Describe ADF’s structural strengths relative to adjacent tool categories — factually.

## Background

Differentiation must come from architecture and operating model already built in
BUILD-001…020, not from slogans.

## Current Situation

RC1 includes documentation-oriented repository SSOT, engine stack, Studio control
center, distribution surfaces, collaboration (AI as participant), planning
orchestration, and governance foundations.

## Analysis

Many tools optimize a single slice (chat, tickets, pipelines, or wikis). ADF’s
difference is integration of documentation, architecture discipline, runtime, and
control-center presentation under one product identity — with humans remaining
decision makers.

## Strategy

| Differentiator | What it means in practice |
|----------------|---------------------------|
| **Documentation-first** | Contracts and docs are product surfaces, not afterthoughts |
| **Architecture-first** | Locked structure and ADRs constrain drift |
| **Business + Engineering SSOT** | `.adf` for engineering; `business/ADF-v1.0` for strategy; `/legal` for product legal |
| **Integrated Studio** | Control center over services/Core — not a detached chat widget |
| **AI Runtime** | Runtime and context machinery for resumable AI-assisted work |
| **Marketplace** | Registry/package pathway for ecosystem distribution foundations |
| **Enterprise governance** | Governance foundations oriented to organizational accountability (hardening continues on roadmap) |
| **Observability** | Runtime monitoring foundations for operational visibility |

Limits to state alongside strengths: orchestration is planning-oriented in RC1;
autonomous multi-agent execution and live production SSO IdP wiring are not RC1 claims.

## Recommendations

1. Lead with structural differentiators, then map to edition needs.
2. Pair each strength with a limit when speaking to enterprise/government.
3. Do not claim uniqueness via superlatives.
