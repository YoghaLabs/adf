# Industry Overview

## Objective

Describe the industry context in which ADF operates — qualitatively, without statistics.

## Background

Software delivery increasingly mixes human engineers with AI assistants. Tooling
spans editors, chat assistants, ticketing, wikis, CI/CD, and internal developer
platforms. Few offerings treat documentation, architecture contracts, and AI
operating state as one repository-native system.

## Current Situation

ADF sits in the emerging space of AI-assisted engineering platforms/frameworks:
closer to developer platforms than to pure chat assistants, while remaining specific
to AI development operating discipline.

## Analysis

Industry pressure points relevant to ADF:

- AI tools raise output speed but often weaken shared project memory
- Enterprises ask for auditability and control as AI use spreads
- Platform engineering seeks standardized internal paths for delivery
- Documentation and knowledge bases lag behind code changes
- Organizations resist replacing every adjacent tool; they add layers carefully

ADF’s industry role is a structured operating layer for AI-assisted development,
complementary to PM, DevOps, and IDE ecosystems.

## Strategy

Frame industry conversations around **continuity, governance readiness, and
documentation-first delivery**, not around displacing every category at once.

## Recommendations

1. In briefings, map ADF next to developer platforms + AI engineering process.
2. Explicitly complement — do not claim to replace — PM and DevOps suites.
3. Keep RC1 lifecycle stage visible in industry discussions.
