# Competitive Landscape

## Objective

Describe the competitive landscape by solution category only — no vendor names.

## Background

Product Strategy `COMPETITIVE_POSITION.md` sets category posture. This document is
the market SSOT expansion for GTM and diligence.

## Current Situation

Buyers shortlist mixed tool stacks. ADF rarely replaces an entire stack; it competes
for the “AI engineering operating layer” slot.

## Analysis

Competition is categorical overlap, not a single peer set. Winning means correct
category framing plus honest non-overlap.

## Strategy

| Category | Buyer expectation | Overlap with ADF | ADF stance |
|----------|-------------------|------------------|------------|
| **AI coding assistants** | Fast in-editor help and chat | Partial (AI-assisted coding) | Not primary identity; framework continuity over chat UX |
| **Project management** | Tasks, status, planning rituals | Low–indirect | Complement; do not replace |
| **Developer platforms** | Shared services, paved paths, APIs | Moderate–high | Closest peer category; ADF is AI-engineering specific framework/platform |
| **Knowledge platforms** | Searchable docs/wikis | Partial | Knowledge tied to architecture/runtime/state — not standalone wiki |
| **Enterprise engineering tools** | Governance, standards, auditability | Moderate | Align on governance narrative; RC1 foundations + roadmap hardening |

**Primary competitive frame:** AI Development Framework / Enterprise AI Engineering Platform.

## Recommendations

1. In bake-offs, insist on continuity, SSOT, and governance criteria.
2. Accept coexistence with assistants, PM, and DevOps tools.
3. Never insert competitor brand names into Business SSOT.

## Implementation Plan

Sales battlecards (later) stay category-based; Marketing avoids vendor call-outs in SSOT.

## Deliverables

Category landscape table for GTM.

## Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Forced assistant feature parity contest | Return to positioning + this landscape |
